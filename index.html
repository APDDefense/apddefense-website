01  /  The problem

When the signal drops, the casualty picture falls apart.

Cell coverage ends where a lot of hard work begins. Casualty information ends up split across radio calls, notebooks and memory, right when a medic has the least time to pull it together.

NO SIGNAL

No towers, no internet

In the backcountry, underground, or anywhere the infrastructure is down, phones lose their data link. Apps that lean on the cloud stop helping.

SCATTERED

Information in pieces

Who’s hurt, how badly, what’s been done and where they are lives in different heads, radios and scraps of paper.

HANDOFF

Every handoff costs time

Building and reading out a MEDEVAC request by hand is slow, and easy to get wrong under stress.

02  /  The PulseLink system

One app. One encrypted radio. No infrastructure.

PulseLink is two parts designed to work as one: an Android app built around how medics triage, and a wearable APD Defense radio that carries team data on its own encrypted sub-1 GHz link.

The PulseLink app

Android · works offline

Triage, treatment, the 9-line and a heading-up plot of where everyone is, all on one screen. No accounts and no server: your team’s data stays on your devices and the radio network.

  • Four-category triage board with a live tally and filters.
  • MARCH checklist and a 9-line MEDEVAC builder.
  • Built-in training mode for realistic practice.

The APD Defense radio

Sub-1 GHz · AES-256

A wearable data radio for places the phone network doesn’t reach. Every packet is encrypted and authenticated.

  • AES-256 authenticated encryption; tampered and replayed packets are rejected.
  • Carries Cursor-on-Target, the format ATAK speaks.
  • Designed to relay traffic across a team mesh and to scale.
  • Casualty telemetry format ready for sensor integration.

03  /  Features

Everything the medic needs, on one screen.

Demonstrated on development hardware; body-worn vital-sign sensors are on the roadmap.

AES-256 · sub-1 GHz

Encrypted radio link

Your team’s data moves over its own encrypted radio link. No cell service or internet required. Tampered or replayed packets are rejected.

Immediate · Delayed · Minimal · Expectant

Triage board

Every casualty in one of four categories, with a live tally and one-tap filters. Each category has its own shape as well as its color, so the board reads at a glance.

M · A · R · C · H

MARCH checklist

Tick through the MARCH sequence for each casualty. It’s the medic’s own record and memory aid, so nothing gets lost when you’re working several at once.

9-line · large-type read-off

MEDEVAC builder

PulseLink drafts the 9-line from what it already knows. You confirm every line, then read it over the net from a large-type card, one line per row.

Your GPS · your compass

Heading-up tactical plot

Up is the way you’re facing. Pick a casualty and see their range and bearing, then turn toward them and go. No data connection needed.

USGS topo · OSM-derived

Offline topo maps

Load the map for your area before you go — USGS topo across the US, OpenStreetMap-derived tiles elsewhere, with the credit shown on the map. Once you’re out, the map needs no network at all.

In-app downloads coming

Cursor-on-Target

ATAK interoperability

The radio carries Cursor-on-Target, the format ATAK speaks. Demonstrated end to end into ATAK-CIV, with a PC bridging handset and radio.

QR scan · 8-digit code

Set up radios by sticker

Every radio carries a sticker. Scan its QR code or type its 8-digit code to assign it to a team member. It works offline, with no numbers to manage.

No accounts · no cloud

Your data stays yours

No analytics, no crash uploads, no update checks, no accounts, no servers. Positions, casualty records and reports stay on your devices and the radio network. The only thing that uses a network is loading map tiles, before you go.

True-black display

Dark by design

A pure-black interface built for OLED screens, where black pixels are switched off. The screen stays dim at night and easy to read in a hurry.

Training mode · 13 built-in scenarios

Train like it’s real.

Thirteen built-in scenarios, from a healthy baseline and minor injuries to a multi-casualty IED. Casualties change over time and some get worse. Simulated casualties are placed around your real position, so trainees walk out and find them. Everything simulated is clearly marked as training.

05  /  How it works

Three steps from injury to handoff.

  1. 01

    Clip on the radio

    Each team member carries an encrypted APD Defense radio. There are no towers, accounts or internet to set up.

  2. 02

    Triage on one screen

    Sort casualties into four categories, work the MARCH checklist and see who’s where on the heading-up plot.

  3. 03

    Hand off fast

    Confirm the 9-line PulseLink drafted, read it over the net from the large-type card, and share positions with ATAK.
